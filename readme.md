# Master's Thesis: Distributed SAR Imaging [with MATLAB Code]

Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) technology achieves high-resolution imaging of land and sea through the synthesis of a virtual long swath by the platform's own movement, offering advantages such as all-weather, all-day, and high resolution. Three-dimensional imaging technology expands the resolution from two dimensions to three dimensions by synthesizing a large antenna swath in height, solving the issues of target overlapping that exist in traditional SAR two-dimensional imaging. However, single-base SAR is limited by the platform's spatial size and hardware scalability, making it difficult to meet the increasingly complex task requirements.
To address these challenges, distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging technology utilizes distributed multi-platforms with separate transmitters and receivers, constructing elevational information for multiple baselines to acquire three-dimensional space targets in a short time and efficiently achieve SAR three-dimensional high-resolution imaging, accomplishing real-time monitoring and three-dimensional reconstruction of ground object scenes.
This paper will conduct theoretical research and experimental verification on key issues such as echo modeling, error analysis, and imaging processing in distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging and point cloud denoising, focusing mainly on the following content: (1) an echo model for distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging has been established, the distance history of targets has been derived, the characteristics of distributed SAR echoes and their three-dimensional resolution capabilities have been analyzed, and the impact of baseline differences on distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging performance has been studied, laying a theoretical foundation for subsequent distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging.
(2) The statistical distribution characteristics of frequency source errors between distributed SAR platforms have been analyzed, and a mathematical model for the impact of time-frequency synchronization errors on distributed SAR imaging has been established, leading to the derivation of constraints on high-precision distributed SAR imaging time-frequency synchronization errors.
(3) In addressing the high-resolution reconstruction issue of distributed SAR 3D imaging, a sparse signal 3D reconstruction model based on Compressed Sensing Theory has been established. The study explores a three-dimensional imaging method for distributed SAR that employs iterative minimization sparse Bayesian reconstruction with likelihood ratio testing. By reducing the false targets and noise in imaging results through the generalized likelihood ratio model, the high-resolution 3D reconstruction of distributed SAR is realized.
(4) For the filtering and denoising problem of point clouds in distributed SAR 3D images, a SAR point cloud filtering method based on spatial cluster seed growth algorithm was studied. Furthermore, a point cloud filtering method based on Principal Component Analysis-Unsupervised Density Clustering was proposed to enhance the efficiency and precision of point cloud extraction. This approach filters out ground vegetation, noise, and false targets from point clouds, thereby improving the quality of distributed SAR 3D imaging.
The above research work has been verified through theoretical analysis and numerical simulations, proving the effectiveness of the proposed theoretical models and processing methods. These can be applied to solve key scientific problems in distributed SAR 3D imaging and point cloud denoising.
This paper conducts in-depth research on the three-dimensional imaging and point cloud denoising methods of distributed SAR. It first derives the echo model and range history for distributed SAR's three-dimensional imaging, introduces the back projection algorithm and tomographic imaging principles to study distributed SAR's two-dimensional and three-dimensional imaging; it analyzes the three-dimensional resolution of distributed SAR signals through analyzing their waveforms and imaging processes, then analyzes the impact of baseline differences and signal carrier frequency on the imaging quality of distributed SAR; subsequently, it analyzes the statistical distribution characteristics of frequency source errors between multiple platforms in distributed SAR, establishes a mathematical model for the impact of time-frequency synchronization errors on distributed SAR imaging, and derives the constraints of high-precision imaging for time-frequency synchronization errors; then, it studies the three-dimensional high-resolution imaging method based on SBRIM-GLRT, which significantly improves the imaging quality compared to matching filtering; finally, it investigates the point cloud denoising noise removal method based on spatial clustering seed growth, and proposes a point cloud denoising extraction method based on Principal Component Analysis (PCA)-DBSCAN, which eliminates noise points and false targets in the three-dimensional point cloud of distributed SAR. The following provides a brief introduction to the six chapters contained in this article:
Chapter 1 is the introduction section, which first briefly elaborates on the research background and significance of distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging and point cloud denoising methods. Subsequently, it introduces the current state of research at home and abroad in three aspects: distributed cluster UAV platforms, dual/multi-base SAR, and SAR three-dimensional imaging processing methods. Finally, it briefly outlines the content and structure of the entire text.
Chapter 2 focuses on the modeling and analysis of echoes in distributed SAR systems. It begins by constructing a spatial geometric model for a multi-receiver, one-transmitter distributed SAR system, from which an echo signal model is derived. Subsequently, gradient methods are employed to solve for the three-dimensional spatial resolution of distributed SAR, with baseline constraints analyzed. Finally, simulations based on the backprojection algorithm and layered imaging principles are conducted to study the impact of baseline differences and signal carrier frequency on three-dimensional imaging quality, verifying that distributed SAR can address the problem of overlapping overlays in two-dimensional imaging.
Chapter 3 investigates the impact of time-frequency synchronization errors on three-dimensional imaging in distributed SAR. It first analyzes the statistical distribution characteristics and types of root frequency source errors that introduce synchronization errors. Then, it establishes mathematical models for the impact of these errors on the quality of distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging, along with error constraints, and validates them through simulation experiments.
Chapter 4 explores the method of distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging based on SBRIM-GLRT. Given the sparse distribution characteristic of imaging targets in elevation direction, a sparse signal model for distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging is established. Subsequently, the theory of compression perception is introduced, and the high-elevation sparse target recovery in distributed SAR is realized using the iterative minimization sparse Bayesian (SBRIM) algorithm. To address the issue of noise and spurious targets in the recovered scene after sparse recovery, the model order selection for sparse signal reconstruction is performed using Generalized Likelihood Ratio Test (GLRT), ultimately achieving high-resolution reconstruction of distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging. The feasibility of the above theoretical methods is verified using simulation data.
Chapter 5 focuses on the research of point cloud denoising processing for distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging. The study first explores the process architecture of the spatial clustering seed growth algorithm and elucidates its underlying principles of breadth-first search, followed by the implementation of filtering and denoising processing for distributed SAR three-dimensional point clouds based on the spatial clustering seed growth algorithm in the absence of precise elevation information. Subsequently, the unsupervised density clustering DBSCAN algorithm is introduced, detailing its algorithmic thought and specific workflow. An optimization of DBSCAN through principal component analysis (PCA) is proposed, leading to a distribution-based SAR three-dimensional point cloud filtering extraction method. This approach filters out false targets and noise data within the point cloud. Finally, simulation experiments are conducted to compare and analyze the filtering and denoising performance of the proposed algorithm, validating the effectiveness of the point cloud filtering extraction method.
Chapter 6 summarizes and looks forward to future research directions. It begins with a summary of the main research content and achievements of the entire paper, followed by an analysis of the current design shortcomings and prospects for future research directions and next steps in distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging and point cloud denoising.
Distributed SAR echo modeling and analysis: Traditional single/double-base SAR constructs two-dimensional imaging results in both time domain and frequency domain, but due to the lack of spatial third dimension information, similar Doppler centroids and bistatic distance history can lead to different scattering objects being present in the same resolution cell in the two-dimensional imaging results, resulting in severe overlap issues in elevation. Therefore, to achieve accurate three-dimensional reconstruction of target scenes, it is necessary to conduct multiple views or multiple radar detections at high elevations to construct synthetic apertures, and then reconstruct elevation information from multiple two-dimensional imaging data sets.
By reconstructing multi-layer 2D imaging results along the elevation, Multi-Azimuth SAR achieves three-dimensional imaging, alleviating the problem of elevation-to-azimuth stacking in 2D imaging and enhancing the readability of imaging results. However, due to its multi-pass imaging mechanism, the time cost is significantly increased, and the time coherence is deteriorated, which interferes with the quality of three-dimensional imaging. Therefore, it is considered to adopt a new SAR three-dimensional imaging scheme to simultaneously obtain multiple sets of two-dimensional SAR imaging results, that is, from single platform multi-pass to multi-payload platform single pass. To improve the coherence of two-dimensional results, a specific configuration is set up for a distributed SAR system with one transmit and multiple receives.
2.1 Distributed SAR Backscatter Model
In the distributed SAR 3D imaging model constructed in this paper, a one-launch, multiple-receiver mode is adopted, and radar transceivers are distributed across the distributed platform. Therefore, the geometric configuration and echo model of this system differ significantly from traditional single or dual-base SAR systems. It is necessary to analyze the distance history characteristics and Doppler characteristics of the echo signals, in order to establish a theoretical foundation for the implementation of distributed SAR 3D imaging.
This chapter first constructs the geometric configuration of a distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging system and then constructs its echo signal model based on this. Subsequently, it conducts research and analysis on the distance history characteristics and Doppler characteristics. Finally, it utilizes the backscattering algorithm and the theory of layered inversion to study the quality of distributed SAR three-dimensional imaging under different baseline arrangements, analyzing its impact.

2.2 Analysis of 3D Resolution in Distributed SAR Space
For the performance assessment of SAR imagery imaging quality, image resolution is undoubtedly paramount. In the field of SAR imaging, it is commonly believed that the main lobe width, which is the -3dB bandwidth of the pulse response function, can reflect the spatial resolution of SAR imaging results [85]. However, for this relatively complex distributed SAR multi-base system configuration in this paper, the pulse response function requires corresponding dual-base SAR imaging and tomographic imaging acquisitions [86], and the solution process is complex and slow and inefficient. Therefore, this section will solve the spatial resolution of the distributed SAR system based on imaging principles through analytical calculation, thereby laying a solid foundation for designing an optimized system configuration and related parameters.
